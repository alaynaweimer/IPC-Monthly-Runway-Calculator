⏱️ Why Runway Matters

Runway is your survival clock. It tells you exactly how long your business can operate if revenue dropped to zero tomorrow.

Knowing your runway isn't pessimistic — it's strategic. It tells you when to push for growth, when to cut costs, and when to panic (or not).

⚠️ The #1 reason businesses fail: They run out of cash. Not because they're unprofitable — but because they didn't see it coming in time to act.

📊 Key Concepts

Runway

Runway (months) = Total Cash Reserves ÷ Monthly Burn Rate

How many months you can operate with zero revenue. This is your "survival time" — the clock starts the moment income stops.

Burn Rate

Burn Rate = Fixed Costs + Variable Costs + Owner's Draw

Gross burn is total monthly spending. Net burn subtracts any revenue you're generating. A business with positive net burn is losing money each month.

Cash Reserve Target

Target Reserve = Monthly Burn × 6 months (minimum)

Most financial advisors recommend keeping 3-6 months of expenses in reserve. For businesses with variable income, aim for 6-12 months.

💼 Pro Tips From the Field

  • Check runway monthly: Don't just calculate once. Make it part of your monthly review — things change fast.
  • Include owner's draw: Many owners forget to include what they pay themselves. If you need to eat, it's an expense.
  • Know your "survival mode" number: Calculate two burn rates — normal operations and bare-bones survival. Know what you could cut in an emergency.
  • Credit lines extend runway (but carefully): Available credit is a buffer, but using it starts a new clock — now you're burning cash AND building debt.
  • Revenue is hopeful, reserves are real: Always calculate runway on reserves alone. Revenue projections are guesses; cash in the bank is fact.
  • Seasonal businesses need more: If your income is seasonal, your reserve target should cover the slow season completely.
